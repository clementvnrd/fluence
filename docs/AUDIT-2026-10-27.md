# Audit 2 (FLU-86): why Clement still dictates with Wispr Flow

Clement, 2026-10-27: Fluence is not reliable enough for daily use; Wispr Flow transcribes much
better. Three studies, all on public or synthetic audio, nothing read from his data:

- [`audit-2026-10-27/competitors.md`](audit-2026-10-27/competitors.md): Wispr Flow, Granola and ~20 other tools, 76 sources.
- [`audit-2026-10-27/accuracy.md`](audit-2026-10-27/accuracy.md): models and techniques measured on `bench/french` on the M2.
- [`audit-2026-10-27/internal.md`](audit-2026-10-27/internal.md): the dictation and meeting pipelines traced in the code.

## What we learned

1. **The gap is only partly the model.** Wispr Flow is cloud-only (its own Canto model, trained with
   RL on real dictations, then a fine-tuned Llama for formatting). Its own posts say names and short
   dictations are settled by **context** (text near the cursor, recipients, app, file names) and the
   user's dictionary. Fluence has the pieces (Accessibility, dictionary, edit watcher, Apple's model)
   but does not wire screen context into a dictation.
2. **Fluence loses words after the model.** At Light, « supprime ça / annule ça / efface ça »
   anywhere deletes the sentence before it (FLU-87); auto-learning fills the dictionary with
   sentence-final words (FLU-88); « Corriger » creates global rules from one-letter fixes (FLU-89);
   text is inserted without a leading space or lower-case mid-sentence (FLU-93).
3. **Two existing options make French worse.** Parakeet's vocabulary boost (English model):
   FLEURS 6.7 → 10.2 %, tech-webcam 11.5 → 18.1 %. Medium cleanup with Apple's model answers
   dictations (tech 53.9 % before the guard, which rejects 89 of 114 outputs) (FLU-91).
4. **What helps, measured** (words wrong, bench subset):

   | | FLEURS | webcam | tech | tech-webcam | wait, 4 s / 44 s |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
   | Parakeet Ultra (today) | 8.7 % | 10.2 % | 9.7 % | 11.5 % | 0.07 / 0.35 s |
   | + constrained name fixer (Apple model) | = | = | 8.9 % | 10.2 % | +1.3 s median |
   | Qwen3-ASR 1.7B with the dictionary as hotwords | 4.8 % | 10.2 % | 6.5 % | 7.9 % | 1.7 / 10.8 s |
   | Whisper large-v3-turbo (MLX, 4-bit) | 9.2 % | 12.6 % | 11.0 % | 12.8 % | — / 6.0 s |

   Not worth it: noise suppression before the model (worse on every set), Voxtral Realtime on the
   M2 (0.5× real time), Qwen3-ASR 0.6B, learned text rules (11.5 → 11.0 %).
5. **The microphone matters as much as a model change:** the webcam a metre away costs ~1/3 more
   errors.
6. **Fine-tuning** needs 30–60 min of corrected dictations with their audio (10–25 % fewer jargon
   errors in the literature). Fluence keeps no audio by design: an opt-in store comes first.
